# The Expired Listing Script — v2

**Goal:** Extend listening time past the 27-second threshold and convert to an in-person listing appointment.

**Method mix:** Tactical empathy (Voss), reverse-selling framing (Mulrenin), neutral-tone problem-awareness (NEPQ/Miner), and a measured 5-touch cadence (because expireds convert on calls 3–5, not call 1).

---

## 1. Pre-dial checklist (30 seconds per lead)

Before the phone rings — research so you sound like a neighbor, not a telemarketer:

- **Property:** address, original list price, price drops, DOM, withdrawal vs. expired.
- **Photos:** 2–3 specific weaknesses (dark interior, staging, bad angles).
- **Comps:** one sold within 0.5 mi in last 60 days, with price-per-sqft delta.
- **Owner:** pull name + nearby sold neighbor's name (for micro-proof later).
- **Breath + smile:** stand up, loosen jaw, smile before touching the keypad. It is audible.

---

## 2. Opener — the pattern interrupt

> "Hey — is this [First]? … Hey [First], this is Daniel Yoon over at eXp. **Before you hang up** — I know exactly what this call looks like, and I'm going to make it worth 30 seconds or we're done. Fair?"

**Why this works:**
- "Before you hang up" is a **pattern interrupt** — it names the thing they're about to do and stops the reflex.
- "Make it worth 30 seconds or we're done" is a **micro-commitment with a defined exit** — their brain calculates "30 seconds is cheap, and I can bail," which is a yes.
- "Fair?" is a **no-oriented close** — either answer ("yeah" or silence) keeps them on the line.

**Pauses are weapons.** After "Fair?" — do not fill the silence. Count to three in your head.

---

## 3. Accusation audit (Voss)

Name every negative thing they're already thinking. When you say it first, they can't use it against you.

> "Full transparency — you don't know me, I'm probably the 20th agent to call this week, and you're exhausted. You're probably convinced the next agent is going to say the same things the last one did. And you're probably thinking, 'here comes another pitch.' … Am I off?"

**Why it works:** Inoculates against the three objections they were about to throw. Ends with a **no-oriented question** — "no, you're not off" keeps them talking.

---

## 4. Label + mirror (discovery, not pitch)

Stay in discovery for 3–5 minutes. The agent who listens longest wins the appointment.

### Opening label
> "It sounds like the last few months on the market were frustrating."

(Pause. Let them vent. Do not interrupt.)

### Mirror their last 1–3 words
Them: "Yeah, our agent barely called us after the first two weeks."
You: "Barely called you after the first two weeks?"

(They will explain more. Every mirror buys you 20–60 more seconds of their talk time — that's the currency.)

### Calibrated discovery questions (ask in order, one at a time)
1. "When you first listed, **what did success look like** to you — price, timeline, moving plans?"
2. "**How did your agent describe the strategy** going in?"
3. "**What do you think actually went wrong?**"
4. "**What's the most frustrating part** of where you ended up?"
5. "If you could do one thing differently, **what would it be?**"

**Rules:**
- Never ask a yes/no question in discovery. "How," "what," and "help me understand" only.
- When they blame price, agree: *"Yeah — almost every expired is a pricing story at the end of the day."* Validation opens the next door.
- Take notes. You will repeat their exact words back in the ask.

---

## 5. Bridge — micro-proof, not pitch

> "That tracks. A seller two streets over on [Nearby Street] told me almost exactly the same thing three weeks ago — same sub-market, same price band. We re-listed at a different number with a different photo-and-first-weekend strategy and it's pending now at [$X]. I'm not going to pitch that over the phone — it'd be half-baked and you deserve the real version."

**Why:** Proof + restraint. Most agents spike the ball here and pitch. You refuse to — which signals you're the pro, not the predator.

---

## 6. The ask — specific, low-stakes, two options

> "Give me **20 minutes at your kitchen table.** I'll bring the actual comps for your floor plan, the three things the sold homes on your street did differently, and a pricing strategy built for *today's* buyer — not 2022's. If it's not useful, I pack up, you never hear from me again. **Saturday at 10, or Sunday at 2 — which works better?**"

**Why two options (not "when are you free?"):** Alternative choice eliminates the "I'll think about it" exit. Their brain picks between the two and skips the bigger decision.

---

## 7. Objection handlers (tactical-empathy rebuilds)

### "We're going to re-list with our old agent."
> "Totally makes sense — you have a relationship. **How are you supposed to know** the next listing won't do what the last one did if the plan is the same? All I'm asking: hear a second opinion for 20 minutes before you sign again. Worst case, it confirms you're making the right call."

(*"How am I/are you supposed to…"* is a calibrated question that forces them to reason — not defend.)

### "We're taking a break / not selling right now."
> "Smart — burnout is real. It sounds like you need a minute to breathe. **Would it be ridiculous** to have me stop by for 15 minutes with a current market read, no listing pitch, so whenever you *do* come back on, you already know your number and the two fixes that move the needle? No cost, no obligation."

(*"Would it be ridiculous"* is Voss's no-oriented question — "no it wouldn't" is a yes without feeling like one.)

### "We're going FSBO."
> "Respect — saving commission is real money, and I'd rather a seller try than regret not trying. **Quick question — do you know what percentage of FSBOs in [county] actually closed in the last 90 days vs. ones that eventually hired an agent?** I'll tell you on the call, but it's a number you want *before* you commit. 20 minutes, I'll walk both paths with you, you pick."

(Frames it as loss avoidance: "before you commit" implies a reversible choice right now, irreversible later.)

### "Just send me some info / email me."
> "I could — but honestly the email version is generic and you've probably seen 40 of them this week. The value is me standing in your living room pulling comps for *your* floor plan. **What would have to be true in that 20 minutes for it to feel like time well spent?**"

(Calibrated question flips them into describing their own acceptance criteria — then you deliver exactly that.)

### "How did you get my number?"
> "Public MLS — your listing contract had it. Totally fair question. If you want off my list I'll take you off right now, no weirdness. **But while I've got you** — worth 20 minutes to at least know what your house is worth today?"

### "The market is bad / nothing is selling."
> "It sounds like you've been watching the headlines, and they're rough. Here's the thing though — homes in your price band in [sub-market] are still going pending in [X] days when they're priced and presented right. **What would change** if you knew exactly what 'priced and presented right' looked like for your place? That's the 20 minutes."

### "We want [unrealistic price]."
> "I hear you — and if I could get you [that price], we'd both be thrilled. **Help me understand** how you landed on that number, because what I present on Saturday might confirm it, or might save you another 90 days on the market. Either way, you leave the meeting with better information than you had this morning."

(Never argue price on the phone. Agree the number *could* be right, and earn the in-person meeting where you'll show the data.)

---

## 8. Voicemail — 3-touch sequence

Expireds convert on **calls 3–5**, not call 1. Most agents quit at call 2. Your edge is stamina + varied touches.

### VM #1 (day 1)
> "Hey [First], Daniel Yoon, eXp Realty — saw your home on [Street] came off the market. Not calling to pitch you. I have **one specific reason** I think it didn't sell and a strategy working right now in [sub-market]. 804-896-2694. If I don't hear back I'll try you tomorrow morning around 9."

### VM #2 (day 2 — if no answer)
> "Hey [First], Daniel again. Two quick things: first, a home almost identical to yours on [Nearby Street] just went pending in [X] days after it re-listed. Second, I promise this isn't a pitch call — it's a 5-minute conversation. 804-896-2694."

### VM #3 (day 5 — the permission close)
> "Hey [First], Daniel, eXp — last one from me, I don't want to be that guy. **If I don't hear back, I'll assume you've got it handled and I'll stop calling.** If something changed, 804-896-2694. Wishing you well either way."

The "permission to stop" VM gets the highest callback rate of the three — loss aversion + you removed the pressure.

---

## 9. Cadence (5 touches over 10 days)

| Day | Touch | Channel |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Call → VM #1 if no answer | Phone |
| 2 | Call → VM #2 if no answer | Phone |
| 3 | Hand-written note mailed to property | Mail |
| 5 | Call → VM #3 | Phone |
| 7 | Text: "Daniel from eXp — left a couple VMs. Totally fine if you're handled. One-time text, no more calls unless you want them." | SMS |
| 10 | Final call, morning | Phone |

After day 10 — drop to a monthly value-touch list (market report, no ask).

---

## 10. Delivery — the 70% of this that isn't words

- **Smile before dialing.** Audibly changes vowel shape.
- **Stand up.** Diaphragm opens. Voice drops ~10 Hz and sounds more authoritative.
- **Mirror their energy *down*.** Expireds are tired. Low, calm, adult > peppy. If they sound annoyed, you get slower and quieter, not faster and louder.
- **Pace: 140–160 WPM max.** Most new agents run 180+. Slow is trust.
- **Pauses are weapons.** After "Fair?" — don't rush. After the ask — don't fill silence. Whoever speaks first loses.
- **Never argue price.** Agree it could be right, earn the appointment.
- **One question at a time.** Stacking questions kills response rate.
- **"That's right" > "you're right."** If you hear "that's right" in the call, you've earned the appointment. Mirror/label until you hear it.

---

## 11. Tracking (non-negotiable)

Log every call. Expireds convert on the 3rd–5th dial — you need the data to prove it to yourself when attempt 2 feels pointless.

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| Name / number / address | Dedupe, compliance |
| Attempt # | Where in cadence |
| Connect? Y/N | Conversion math |
| Talk time (sec) | Your north-star metric — optimize for this, not appointments |
| Objection raised | Which handler needs work |
| Handler used | A/B test your own language |
| Next action + date | Never let a lead sit cold |
| Outcome | Appt set / DNC / nurture |

**Primary KPI: average talk time per connect.** Conversions are downstream of listening time. If your average goes up week over week, appointments follow automatically.

---

## 12. What changed vs. v1, and why

| v1 | v2 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "May I have 27 seconds…" | "Before you hang up — I'll make it worth 30 seconds or we're done. Fair?" | Pattern interrupt + defined exit → higher listen-through rate |
| Single reverse-pitch question | 5-question calibrated discovery + mirrors + labels | Buys 3–5× more talk time, which is the actual conversion lever |
| No accusation audit | Explicit accusation audit up front | Defuses the 3 objections they were about to throw |
| Objections answered with logic | Objections answered with "how are you supposed to…" / "would it be ridiculous…" | Calibrated + no-oriented questions outperform logic rebuts |
| One voicemail | 3-VM sequence ending in permission close | Callback rate is highest on VM #3, not VM #1 |
| No cadence | 5-touch 10-day cadence across call/mail/text | Matches the call-3–5 conversion reality |
| "Track every call" | Specific tracking schema + north-star KPI (talk time) | You improve what you measure — and talk time is upstream of appointments |
