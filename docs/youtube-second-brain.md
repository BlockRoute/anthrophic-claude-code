## 1. Understand new, casual & regular viewers

**Source:** https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10246996

YouTube segments your audience into three groups based on how frequently they
watch your channel:

- **New viewers** — watched your content for the first time in the selected period.
- **Casual viewers** — have watched your channel occasionally over time.
- **Regular viewers** — your most loyal, frequently-returning audience.

### Why it matters

- Channels with a healthy base of **returning viewers** tend to be favored by
  YouTube's recommendation system, which helps surface content to new people.
- Your **monthly audience can look smaller than your subscriber count** — many
  subscribers are casual or lapsed, so active monthly viewers is a truer signal
  of engagement than raw subscriber totals.

### How to grow regular viewers

- **Upload consistently** on similar topics or in a familiar, repeatable format.
- **Use community-building features** — Community posts, comments, live
  premieres, and live chats.
- **Keep branding consistent** across thumbnails, intros, and channel art so
  returning viewers instantly recognize your content.

## 2. Video tips — basic features & production

**Source:** https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12931897
(sections: `#zippy=basic-features` and `#zippy=production`)

Guidance for creating stronger videos. Success does **not** require the most
expensive setup — many top creators use a phone or webcam — but a few
fundamentals disproportionately improve quality.

### Production quality

- **Lighting** has more impact on visual quality than almost any other factor.
  Use natural light when possible, or add a softbox, ring light, or LED panel to
  remove harsh shadows and give footage a bright, polished look.
- **Audio is non-negotiable.** Viewers forgive imperfect visuals far sooner than
  bad sound. An external microphone dramatically cuts background noise and
  captures clean, clear dialogue.
- You don't need the highest-end camera — good lighting and audio on basic gear
  beat a great camera with poor sound.

### Basic features (discoverability)

- **Titles** — short, bold, and keyword-driven; make the value obvious.
- **Thumbnails** — the first visual impression; keep them eye-catching and
  genuinely representative of the video (avoid misleading clickbait).
- **Descriptions & tags** — add relevant keywords to the title, description, and
  tags to help ranking in YouTube search and suggested feeds.

## 3. Upload schedule tips for Shorts

**Source:** https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13616979 (`YTVideoType=shorts`)

Once you know *what* content you'll create and *how often*, turn that into an
upload schedule for the coming weeks or months.

### Factors to weigh when building a schedule

- **Frequency** — how many Shorts per day/week/month, and whether that pace is
  **sustainable** long term.
- **Consistency** — being dependable about both your upload cadence and your
  content themes.
- **Structure** — a clear plan for what content comes when.

### Production tactics for high frequency

- **Batch filming** — record several Shorts in one session.
- **Save drafts** — keep in-progress Shorts as drafts to publish later.
- **Repurpose** — turn clips of your long-form videos into Shorts.
- **Reply with Shorts** — respond to channel comments with a Short. This is a
  good source of high-frequency, low-production-value content.
- Use built-in **YouTube Shorts tools** to speed up creation and uploading.

### Consistency is the core principle

A consistent, sustainable release schedule is critical for building and meeting
audience expectations — *and* for protecting your own well-being. Pick a pace you
can actually maintain rather than an aggressive one you'll burn out on.

### Timing (general patterns — always validate against your own data)

- Evenings, roughly **6–9 p.m.**, tend to perform well for Shorts.
- Mid-week and weekend days are often strong.
- These are only starting points. Layer your **audience's country and content
  type** on top, then tune using the **"When your viewers are on YouTube"** chart
  in YouTube Studio, which reflects your actual audience's habits.

## 4. Tips for Advanced Mode on Analytics

**Source:** https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16766491

Advanced Mode (the "See More" / Advanced Mode view in YouTube Analytics) lets you
slice performance data far more deeply than the default reports.

### Custom groups

- Create **groups** — customizable collections of up to **500** of your videos,
  playlists, or channels — to analyze related content together in one place.
- Organize groups by **content pillars**, **editing style/tone**, or **video
  length** to see how each cohort performs collectively rather than one video at
  a time.

### Filters

Narrow data for deeper insight. Useful filters include:

- **Geography** — where your viewers are.
- **Device** — how they watch.
- **Content type** — isolate Shorts vs. long-form performance.
- **Traffic source** — find which videos win from external traffic.
- **Title** — locate specific videos quickly.

### Comparisons

- Click **Add Comparison** to compare different content, date ranges, or metrics
  side by side.
- Useful date ranges: last **28 days** (real-time pulse), **90 days / 6 / 12
  months** (seasonal trends), **lifetime** (overall channel health), and **custom
  ranges** for year-over-year analysis.

### Save your work

- Save a **custom view** that bundles your go-to filters and dimensions so you can
  return to the same report without rebuilding it each time.
